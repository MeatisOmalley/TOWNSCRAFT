# DOScraft optimization priorities — 2026-10-11

This is a **ranked research backlog**, not a set of enabled changes or promised FPS gains. It combines the proposals from the DOScraft optimization conversations, the [DOS backlog](OPTIMIZATION_BACKLOG.md), [performance record](PERFORMANCE.md), [front-to-back experiment](FRONT_TO_BACK.md), [group-visibility audit](research/group_visibility/FEASIBILITY.md), and the Townscraft [retention audit](../../fmtowns/townscraft/OPTIMIZATION_AUDIT.md). The older Townscraft discussions also raised interlacing, streaming, cache use, and period hardware. The ranking favors **Low** and useful moving/doorway scenes; High may retain a cleaner reference path. A lower rank can be easier to implement or a better first diagnostic.

## Pre-flip baseline and how to read the ranking

The 486DX/25, 16 MiB, ET4000AX ISA/Mode X replay before the async flip measured the following means. “Still” and “walking” are **different camera views and scene workloads**; their difference is not the isolated cost of player movement. The benchmark freezes much of normal simulation and is not a live-play FPS claim. [Low report](tests/benchmarks/2026-10-11/proven-vertical-low.json) · [High report](tests/benchmarks/2026-10-11/proven-vertical-high.json) · [VM configuration](runtime/platform-486dx25-xecixfz2/86box.cfg)

| Scene | Frame | Draw | VGA present | World update | Present detail |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Low still | 67.4 ms | 32.3 ms (48%) | 29.3 ms (44%) | ~0.1 ms | pack 4.9, upload 14.7, retrace 9.4 ms |
| Low walking | 52.6 ms | 10.5 ms (20%) | 25.5 ms (49%) | 9.0 ms (17%) | pack 5.1, upload 10.8, retrace 9.2 ms |
| High still | 136.5 ms | 94.9 ms (70%) | 34.0 ms (25%) | 0.9 ms | full-resolution draw dominates |
| High walking | 102.7 ms | 43.1 ms (42%) | 37.6 ms (37%) | 12.1 ms | both draw and present matter |

Within sampled Low drawing, the opaque texel loop accounts for about 34–35% of draw samples, scanline/gradient setup 15–26%, polygon edge scanning about 13–14%, and face projection/setup 8–14%. These are **sample shares within draw**, not frame-time savings. The current loop is already 486 assembly with four-texel unrolling. [Sampler](tests/benchmarks/2026-10-11/current-low-draw-profile.json) · [Raster assembly](vendor/towns/src/trap.S)

Low still submits only ~19,300 logical pixels per frame (about 1.2× the 160×100 view), so average spans are short and **per-row and per-polygon setup** cost about as much as texels. Row setup and edge scanning scale with the number of rows, not the number of columns. Other Low stage means not shown above: collect/sort 1.5 (still), 3.6 (walk), 6.2 (down) and 8.3 ms (house/edits); sky 2.3–5.2 ms; finish (odd-row copy) 1.4–2.3 ms. Within present, the HUD shadow comparison is ~6% of samples. [Present sampler](tests/benchmarks/2026-10-11/current-low-present-functions.json)

**Retrace quantization in the baseline.** The former DOS presenter waited for retrace before every flip ([video.c](src/platform/dos/video.c)). The Towns original flipped three pages without waiting ([Towns gfx.c](vendor/towns/src/gfx.c)). Thus baseline frame times snapped to whole 60 Hz refreshes: Low still was 67.42 ms with max 67.48 ms, exactly four refreshes. This is why several earlier savings measured “neutral, retrace absorbs it”. It was a property of the former presenter, not of the hardware; see candidate #1.

“Expected impact” below is a relative judgment about potential **whole-frame** improvement on the current workload, not a measured percentage. The shipped synchronous-flip baseline could absorb work saved before retrace; #1 now removes that wait. Quality and scope changes are marked explicitly; proposing them here does not authorize implementation. Rows marked **NEW (2026-10-11 review)** were added after a source and stage-timing review; they have not been measured.

The invisible #1 and #5 changes have separate guest measurements. #2 and the first step of #3 now have guest measurements too. Their gains overlap, so use the combined Very Low report rather than adding individual savings.

Other low-confidence backlog investigations remain available after hotspot evidence: narrower fixed-point formats; selective perspective-correct intervals if they allow *less* subdivision; additional transform reuse beyond the existing additive camera tables; projected-size culling; x87 batching; and entity/light scheduling. Perspective correction usually **adds** work if subdivision is unchanged. Seed-plus-edits storage, larger-world generation and saving are architecture/capacity projects, not near-term frame-time optimizations. [Detailed inventory](OPTIMIZATION_BACKLOG.md)

**Measurement gap.** The replay freezes physics and mobs, so mob AI/physics and entity box drawing during real play are unmeasured (benchmark entity stage ≈0). A live-simulation replay, or a profiled real-input session, should precede any ranking of simulation or entity work. A Low mob-count or entity-LOD cap would be a gameplay-affecting option to evaluate only after that.

## What is already done, and what should not be retried unchanged

The release already has affine **16.16 fixed-point** texturing, reciprocal lookup tables, greedy/exposed-face meshes, frustum/backface rejection, additive camera tables, adaptive subdivision in every preset, four-texel assembly unrolling, Low/Medium producer-proven horizontal and vertical repeated-row packing/broadcast, per-page HUD caching, High ET4000 banked upload, direct pitched High rows, and word-wise HUD comparison. “Use affine mapping,” “rewrite the C texture loop in assembly,” “add DDA for block targeting,” and “use a faster HUD cache” therefore do not describe missing work. [DOS performance record](PERFORMANCE.md) · [Towns retention audit](../../fmtowns/townscraft/OPTIMIZATION_AUDIT.md)

Measured losing or neutral checkpoints include exact front-to-back coverage (two variants), wall-cone item rejection, the old coarse occlusion prepass, full-frame compact-buffer expansion, exact edge-reciprocal substitution, word transpose, fused Low pack/classification (two variants), shared-corner projection reuse, exact-zero/stable gradient setup, depth-aware 2×2 leaf changes, and first-plane group facing (roughly neutral). Preserve their diagnostics, but do not rank the **same implementation** as untried. A successor above names its materially different mechanism. [Measured history](PERFORMANCE.md) · [Coverage experiment](FRONT_TO_BACK.md)

## Test order and decision rules

1. With #1 validated on the ET4000 High path and save/reload smoke, re-measure checkpoints recorded as neutral or retrace-quantized (for example group facing, stable gradient, fused packing) before writing them off.
2. The `raster_finish` skip is measured and enabled. Prototype #3's remaining direct plane-layout rasterizer separately before combining it with other draw changes.
3. The #2 output path is enabled only in Very Low. Any next 80-column rasterizer experiment should retain sharp HUD/overlay fallbacks and compare moving and doorway images as well as still frames.
4. Separately instrument #7 unchanged rows by **destination page** on still, walking, house and edits before writing a complex dirty-run uploader. Coalescing cost must be below saved ISA time.
5. Take the census before the change: count invalidation reasons for #8 and mergeable side quads for #13. Count eligible texels/quads for #11–12 while testing #10. Implement a specialization only if its eligibility is high enough to pay dispatch and cache cost.
6. Profile #15 in walking and edits, including p95/max and edit-to-visible latency. Evaluate #16, #17 and #21 in private, historically valid VM configurations; do not silently replace the player VM or the ET4000-specific High path.
7. Use the current production flags, fixed seed/route and Low-first scoring. The default three repeats now share one generated world and include an unreported fixture warmup; `--separate-processes` retains independent-world validation. Do not compare these different replay contexts as equivalent. Compare **frame, draw, pack, upload, retrace, world update, byte counts and visual evidence**. Keep the doorway route and High reference; test save/reload and VGA readback for any adopted presenter.

The list is ordered by plausible impact, **not** permission to change the UI or game logic. In particular, a smaller viewport, altered controls/HUD, or broad gameplay/storage redesign would need a separate explicit request before implementation. This file itself makes no such changes.
